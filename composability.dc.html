Case study · FRAMEWORK

Scale cross-product composability

Standardized modular feature rollouts and sped up cross-app integration across 3 core products while preserving quality in customer experiences.
Role
Staff Systems Designer, Page Architecture & Composability
Partners
Engineering Architects, Platform Engineering, Senior Designers (Admin, Commerce, Identity)
Timeline
Under 2 years, 2023–2025
Scope
Architecture, layout, framework, operating model

Overview

In 2023, cross-surface user experiences at Adobe faced severe fragmentation: teams built bespoke, single-product experiences that made cross-app workflows expensive to build and brittle to maintain. The desired outcome is unification, composability, and scale across Adobe experiences. In the first leap, I led the design architecture of a unified Page Framework across two levels of abstraction: decoupling UI patterns from business capabilities, and capabilities from host applications. This model was proven by scaling Business Essentials (a shipped Fall 2023 Administration workflow containing key capabilities) across Adobe Express and Acrobat with zero breaking framework changes thru 2025, over a year later.

With this validation, scaling composability at quality across Adobe became an executive-level priority. The next goal of this charter became "how do we scale this to the three Platform experiences: Identity, Administration, and Commerce?".

The challenge: 3 divergent platform realities

To unlock reliable, quality, and durable cross-product composability as a platform org, the people in these teams needed to approach this work uniformly. I led the unification of three conflicting engineering and design cultures.
Administration: Building new, scalable workflows with partial component reuse.
Identity: Strictly locked down by security protocols and authorization compliance, with zero tolerance for visual drift.
Commerce: Rapidly experimenting with custom, high-conversion layouts that resisted shared constraints.
Bridging these silos meant establishing shared operational principles, defining acceptable parameters for layout deviation, and co-creating expectations for the product designer's role in composable systems.

The solution: A Two-Part Approach to Composability

1. The "How We Work" Model (Organizational Alignment)

Shared Principles: Co-created elevated operational standards across Admin, Commerce, and Identity to replace isolated product roadmaps. (Cross-functional, cross-team)
The Designer’s Role in Composability: Co-defined clear expectations and workflows for product designers, shifting their focus from designing static, single-use pages to authoring modular, reusable capabilities following quality standards. (Cross-team)

2. The Technical Architecture (The Composability Engine)

Container-Agnostic Architecture: Decoupled structural rules across Foundation, Orchestration, Capability, and Surface tiers.
Unified Layout: Delivered full-page and centered dialog layouts in the initial release via PageTemplate, with built-in extensibility for future embedded and inline UI.
Rules of Composition: Established guardrails defining which capabilities could combine, how modules fit together, and where layout flexibility was allowed.
Cross-Product Portability: Capabilities were built once to work across multiple hosts.

Impact and durability

Zero-Rewrite Multi-App Parity: Maintained total architectural stability across major host deployments into Adobe Express and Acrobat.
Cultural Shift at Scale: Moved cross-functional platform teams from building in isolated product silos to authoring shared, nestable capabilities.
Extensible Foundation: Established the design taxonomy and system guardrails required to scale beyond pages into side panels, drawers, and inline surfaces without breaking core logic.
