The problem

GenStudio sat at the intersection of two major Adobe orgs with differing technical foundations, Digital Media and Experience Cloud. Early conceptual iterations prioritized rapid executive buy-in over existing platform architecture, committing to Spectrum 2 before most of the core tokens and web components were production-ready. To hit aggressive weekly milestones, feature teams built bespoke UI, in most cases Spectrum 1 was Express skinned under the guise of Spectrum 2, accumulating massive technical and design debt.

In June 2024, four months before the General Availability Release, I was brought in as the dedicated systems lead to stabilize GenStudio’s foundation and de-risk the launch.

Approach

My goal was to clear up the ambiguity without stalling ongoing feature delivery.
The initial work wasn’t visual design. It was figuring out what was true. Before drawing a single component, I audited the gap between executive expectations and technical feasibility, determining which decisions were locked, what our platforms could actually support, and what adopting Spectrum 2 looked like in practice.
My work breaks down into five phases:
  1. System & Workflow Audit: Investigated how cross-org design and engineering teams were operating day-to-day. Uncovered un-tokenized code, duplicate patterns, and technical debt, then presented a short gap analysis to leadership to reset launch expectations around Spectrum 2.
  2. Component Triage (Keep, Redefine, Discard): Evaluated in-flight UI candidates against objective criteria: intent to reuse, platform overlap, implementation timelines, and necessity. If a pattern was critical but missing upstream, GenStudio built a temporary bridge component with a clear deprecation plan once platform versions landed.
  3. Roadmap & Dependency Alignment: In parallel, actively balanced our deliverables against the evolving roadmaps of Quarry and the core Spectrum 2 platform. This meant negotiating component ownership to ensure my 6 engineers only built what wasn’t already being solved upstream.
  4. Production Engineering with Core Standards: Designed and built the surviving components directly alongside our dedicated engineering team, holding every asset to our standards for craft, accessibility, and ease of use for engineers.
  5. Decoupled Governance & Handoff: Wrote the standards defining core standards, criteria for when local deviation was justified, and clear engagement models across Digital Media and Experience Cloud teams so that GenStudio’s design systems could scale coherently without depending on me.

Defining the components and behavior expectations

Before aligning with Spectrum design, Quarry/React Spectrum engineering, and Brand team for GenStudio requirements, I needed to understand how the GenStudio team was building. I set out to find common components in GenStudio to start a stable foundation. I audited dozens of in-flight design files that had key repeating one-off instances and found engineering implementation doing the same and skinned Spectrum 1 as Express while labeling it Spectrum 2.
I did not set out to meet a specific number of components, they emerged naturally in this process. Key components identified were:
  • The application shell and layout: global top and left navigation, modal dialogs, modal headers
  • The asset discovery and presentation: cards, card gallery (masonry or standard grid), empty states
  • The direct manipulation & canvas: In-line editing patterns for marketing collateral on canvas and artifact metadata
Beyond static UI specs, I defined how components behave and how type scales, so they stayed accessible and predictable:
  • Interaction & Feedback Models: Standardized selection states, multi-action card behaviors (hover/focus/bulk select), and contextual error handling.
  • Enterprise Density & Typography: Defined type scale relationships tailored for a dense, productivity-focused enterprise authoring.
I closed the loop by feeding these edge cases directly back to the core Spectrum team or the Quarry team. Each issue was either fixed upstream or added to their backlog.

Outcome

A cohesive, modern, and trustworthy GenStudio
Unifying disparate Digital Media and Experience Cloud feature sets under a single design language reduced cognitive load, delivering a simplified, on-brand experience for marketers navigating end-to-end workflows.
A cultural shift in zero-to-one product development
GenStudio served as a high-stakes case study in the risks of siloed zero-to-one development. Initially driven by aggressive, ad-hoc execution, it quickly revealed why deep platform knowledge is non-negotiable when building net-new products on top of existing ecosystems. By stepping back to audit and integrate existing platform architecture, we proved that speed does not have to come at the expense of systemic craft or quality. This established a new operational benchmark for how Adobe builds zero-to-one products across organizational boundaries.
Executive and stage validation at Adobe MAX
Digital Media and Experience Cloud leadership recognized GenStudio as their benchmark standard for design execution, stable enough to be demoed live straight from production without bespoke marketing fallbacks.
